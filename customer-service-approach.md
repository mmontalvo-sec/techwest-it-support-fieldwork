# Customer Service Approach

Customer service is one of the strongest skills developed through three years of running Tech West PC Repair. Most clients arrive frustrated, worried about losing data, or confused about why their device stopped working. Handling those conversations well is as important as fixing the device itself.

This document covers the specific practices that guide every client interaction.

---

## Core Principles

| Principle | What It Means in Practice |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| Listen first | Let the client describe the issue fully before asking questions or offering opinions |
| No jargon | Translate every technical concept into plain language a non-technical user can act on |
| Manage expectations | State clearly what is likely, what is possible, and what is uncertain |
| No false promises | Especially for data recovery, never claim an outcome that is not guaranteed |
| Document everything | Provide written summaries so the client has a clear record of the work |
| Educate without lecturing | Briefly explain what caused the issue so the client can recognize warning signs in the future |
| Stay calm | Frustrated clients calm down when the technician stays calm and focused |

---

## Translating Technical Concepts

A large part of the work is converting technical findings into language the client can actually use. The same information delivered differently produces a very different outcome.

### Example 1: Slow PC

Avoid:
> Your HDD has high latency and poor random read performance.

Use:
> Your old drive is slowing down the computer. Replacing it with an SSD will make startup, app loading, and file access much faster. The drive itself is not failing yet, but it is the main reason the computer feels slow.

### Example 2: Ransomware

Avoid:
> The files are encrypted by ransomware.

Use:
> The files are still visible, but their contents were locked or damaged by malware. Standard recovery tools cannot restore them without a usable backup or a valid decryption key. I will document what I found and walk you through what we can and cannot do from here.

### Example 3: Failing Hardware

Avoid:
> SMART reports reallocated sectors and pending sectors above threshold. Drive failure is imminent.

Use:
> The drive is starting to fail. It still works for now, but it is not reliable. The safest move is to copy your important files off it today and replace the drive before it stops working completely.

### Example 4: Aged Laptop

Avoid:
> The CPU is two generations behind current low-end mobile processors. Performance will remain limited regardless of optimization.

Use:
> This laptop is older, so even after we clean it up and upgrade the drive, it will not feel like a brand new computer. It will be noticeably faster than before, and good for everyday use like browsing, email, and documents, but heavy work like video editing will still be slow.

## Managing Expectations

The most important skill in client-facing work is being honest about what can and cannot be done before the work starts. This applies most heavily to:

| Scenario | How Expectations Are Set |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Data recovery | "I will try to recover the files. I cannot guarantee that any will be recovered. I will not charge for recovery if nothing is recovered." |
| Ransomware impact | "Standard tools usually cannot recover encrypted files without the original key. We will look at backups first, and I will tell you honestly what we can do." |
| Old hardware | "We can make this laptop usable, but it will not feel like a new one. I want you to know that before we spend money on parts." |
| Intermittent issues | "If the problem only happens sometimes, the first thing we need to do is identify when it happens, which may take a few days." |
| Unknown root cause | "I am not sure yet what is causing this. I will run a series of tests, and we will know more in a day. I will not guess just to give you an answer." |

## Handling Frustrated Clients

Frustrated clients are not difficult clients. They are stressed clients. The approach is:

1. Acknowledge the frustration briefly without making it the focus.
2. Restate the issue back so they know they have been heard.
3. Move directly into a plan: what will be checked, when, and what they should expect next.
4. Avoid promising specific outcomes until diagnostics are complete.
5. Keep them updated even when there is nothing new to report.

What works is calm steadiness. What does not work is matching their energy or making excuses.

## Post-Service Communication

Every job concludes with three deliverables:

1. **A demonstration.** Show the client that the original issue is resolved, with them present when possible.
2. **A written summary.** A short service report covering what was done, what parts were replaced, and what was validated.
3. **Recommendations.** Practical next steps, especially around backups and prevention.

The recommendations should always include an honest answer to the question every client implicitly asks: "What should I do so this does not happen again?"

## Client Education

A significant portion of the value Tech West PC Repair provides is education. Clients who understand why a problem happened are clients who are more likely to prevent it in the future and more likely to call for help early next time, when issues are still small.

Education is short, specific, and tied to the issue at hand:

- After a ransomware case: how phishing typically works, why a backup is the only reliable defense.
- After an SSD upgrade: what an SSD is and why it makes a difference.
- After a printer fix: how to check the print queue and restart the spooler when prints get stuck.
- After a thermal cleaning: why dust matters and how often the cleaning should be repeated.

The goal is not to turn the client into a technician. The goal is to make sure the client leaves more confident than they arrived.
